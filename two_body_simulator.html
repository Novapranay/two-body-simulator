Why is this conserved?

  • Energy: gravity is a conservative force (F = −∇U with U = −Gm₁m₂/r), so the work it does depends only on the end points and KE + PE stays constant.
  • Angular momentum about the COM: gravity is a central force along r = r₂ − r₁, so the internal torques cancel: τ = r×F = 0 ⇒ dL/dt = 0. Geometrically this is Kepler’s 2nd law, dA/dt = L/(2μ).
  • Linear momentum: the only forces are the internal pair F₁₂ = −F₂₁ (Newton’s 3rd law) and there is no external force, so dP/dt = 0 and the COM moves at constant velocity (here: stays at rest).

Thrust is an external impulse on body 2 ⇒ E, L and P all change. Drag is an internal but non-conservative, non-central force pair ⇒ P stays conserved, E and L decay (orbit spirals in).

Geosynchronous / geostationary orbits
deg
km
Earth-fixed frame ground track (longitude relative to the red ground station vs latitude):

A geosynchronous orbit has period = one sidereal day (86 164.1 s). If it is also circular, equatorial and prograde it is geostationary: the satellite hangs over one point. Raise it (Δr > 0) and its period gets longer ⇒ it drifts west; lower it ⇒ east. With inclination i the satellite spends half a day north and half south of the equator while its east–west speed relative to the ground oscillates, so the ground track is a figure-8 (analemma) spanning ±i in latitude; eccentricity makes the 8 lopsided.

Theory

1. Newton’s law of gravitation [EQ-1, EQ-2]

F₂₁ = −G m₁ m₂ (r₂ − r₁) / |r₂ − r₁|³ , F₁₂ = −F₂₁ , G = 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² m₁ r̈₁ = F₁₂ , m₂ r̈₂ = F₂₁

Both bodies are integrated explicitly (8 state variables: x, y, vx, vy for each body).

2. Reduced mass — one-body equivalent [EQ-12, EQ-20]

r = r₂ − r₁ , M = m₁ + m₂ , μ = m₁m₂/M r̈ = r̈₂ − r̈₁ = −G(m₁+m₂) r/r³ ⇔ μ r̈ = −G m₁ m₂ r / r³ r₁ = R − (m₂/M) r , r₂ = R + (m₁/M) r

The two-body problem = a fictitious particle of mass μ orbiting a fixed mass M, plus a COM R that moves uniformly. That is why the orbit helper uses GM with M = m₁+m₂, and the energies are E = ½Mv_R² + ½μv² − Gm₁m₂/r.

3. Energy, vis-viva and the conic [EQ-5…EQ-7, EQ-13…EQ-19]

ε = E/μ = v²/2 − GM/r (specific orbital energy, COM frame) v² = GM (2/r − 1/a) (vis-viva) h = r × v , e = [(v² − GM/r) r − (r·v) v] / GM , p = h²/GM r(ν) = p / (1 + e cos ν) , a = −GM/(2ε) , r_p = p/(1+e) , r_a = p/(1−e) circle e = 0 · ellipse 0 < e < 1 (ε < 0) · parabola e = 1 (ε = 0) · hyperbola e > 1 (ε > 0)

4. Kepler’s three laws [EQ-16, EQ-23]

  • 1st: bound orbits are ellipses with the other body (or the COM) at a focus.
  • 2nd: equal areas in equal times: dA/dt = |r × v|/2 = L/(2μ) = const (toggle “Kepler equal-area sweep”).
  • 3rd: T² = 4π² a³ / (G(m₁+m₂)) — note M is the total mass.

5. Geosynchronous radius [EQ-21]

circular orbit: G M m / r² = m ω² r , ω = 2π / T_sid ⇒ r_geo = (G M T_sid² / 4π²)^(1/3) ≈ 42 164 km (altitude ≈ 35 786 km) v_geo = √(GM / r_geo) = ω r_geo ≈ 3.075 km/s

T_sid = 86 164.1 s (one rotation relative to the stars), not 86 400 s (solar day, which includes the extra turn needed because Earth moves around the Sun).

6. Numerical integration [EQ-3, EQ-4]

Velocity-Verlet: v½ = v + a(x)·dt/2 ; x' = x + v½·dt ; v' = v½ + a(x')·dt/2 RK4: four slope evaluations, weights 1/6, 2/6, 2/6, 1/6

Verlet is symplectic and time-reversible: its energy error oscillates but does not grow, even over thousands of orbits. RK4 is more accurate per step but its energy error drifts secularly — set “Resolution” to 100 steps/orbit to watch the difference.
